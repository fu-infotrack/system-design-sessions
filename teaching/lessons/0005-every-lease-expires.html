Locking · Lesson 5 · ~8 minutes

Every lease expires while you hold it

You are already using several of these, and most of them are not called locks.

The knowledge

Any lock that survives the death of its holder needs a time limit — otherwise a crashed process would hold it forever. And the moment it has a time limit:

The core idea

It can expire while you are still working. Not because anything went wrong — just because your work took longer than the number. A garbage-collection pause is enough.

Then someone else acquires it, legitimately, and now two of you are running. Neither is told. Our demo does exactly this with a textbook-correct Redis lock:

dotnet run 07-expiry.cs

expected counter: 8
actual counter:   4
lost updates:     4

The category is much bigger than "locks"

Here is the part worth taking away. Any system that hands you work with a timeout and reassigns it if you do not confirm in time is a lease — and has this failure. You probably meet it in a queue long before a lock:

Microsoft's own Service Bus documentation describes the failure without euphemism, and draws the conclusion this course keeps arriving at:

If Complete fails, which typically occurs at the very end of message handling and in some cases after minutes of processing work, the receiving application can decide whether to preserve the state of the work and ignore the same message when it's delivered for a second time… Designing for idempotent message handling becomes critical. — Microsoft Learn, Service Bus locks and settlement

The trap everyone falls into

Processing is slow, so you raise the timeout. But Service Bus spells out the cost: "when your client stops working, the message becomes available again only after the lock duration passes."

So a longer lease means slower recovery from a genuinely dead worker, and a shorter one means more zombies. You are choosing between duplicate work and delayed work, and no number escapes the choice. Renewal — Service Bus auto-renew, Kafka's background heartbeat — narrows the window without closing it, because the renewal and the work stop at different moments.

The practice

And one about what to do

What to take away

Two habits:

  1. When you see a timeout on something that hands you work, read it as a lock you did not know you had, and find out what happens if your handler overruns it.
  2. Never answer that risk with a bigger number. Answer it by making the second run harmless.
